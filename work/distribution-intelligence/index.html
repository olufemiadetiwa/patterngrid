Approach
  1. Stock model. Warehouse stock, in-transit quantities and outlet-level availability share one product and location model. Units and assumptions are written down alongside the sample data.
  2. Sales view. Sell-in and sell-out are compared by territory and outlet, with the difference shown explicitly.
  3. Exception queue. A rule flags outlets where demand exceeds available stock and assigns each exception to a review owner.

Discover → Design → Build → Validate → Hand over — the Business Intelligence approach.

How the data moves

Sales, inventory and outlet sources → integration → quality gate → curated model → availability view → exception queue.

Designed to enable
Earlier visibility of availability gaps by outlet
A shared definition of demand versus shipments
A review route with named ownership

What the system is built to make possible, not a measured result.

Limitation

What this does not prove

Availability is only as good as the last count. Outlets not visited in the week are excluded and the exclusion is shown rather than estimated.

Technology (illustrative options)

SQL and a lakehouse-style curated model · Power BI for the investigation view · Scheduled pipelines with exception handling
