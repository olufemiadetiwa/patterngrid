What this is

Give every important number a clear meaning.

A dashboard is only useful when the people using it understand the measures, trust the source and know which question to ask next. Different definitions, inconsistent filters and disconnected reports can make a polished interface difficult to use for a real decision.

Pattern Grid brings metric design, data modelling and reporting into one connected piece of work. We agree what performance means, build views around the audience's decisions and make it possible to inspect the numbers behind an exception. The aim is a management meeting where everyone is looking at the same number and can see where it came from.

This is useful when
  • Departments disagree on the same KPI
  • Executive reports require extensive manual preparation
  • Users cannot explain a chart's figures
  • Existing dashboards attract little meaningful use
What you are working towards

Numbers that agree across departments, reports built around real questions, and a routine for finding the cause and assigning someone to act.

Worked example

Why is net sales 4% behind plan this period?

  1. 01Gross sales 10.0m less returns 0.4m equals net sales 9.6m (sample units). Against a plan of 10.0m the variance is −0.4m, or −4%.
  2. 02Before exploring products or regions, the return treatment, reporting period and source of the measure are inspected in the metric card.
  3. 03The next question to investigate is whether returns are concentrated in one product line. Follow-up owner: Commercial finance lead.

What you receive

We agree the exact deliverables with you around your priorities, your systems and where you are starting from.

KPI dictionary

Clear definitions for the agreed measures, including calculation rules, reporting periods, owners, exclusions and the business decisions each measure is intended to support.

Shared calculation model

A reusable model that brings relationships and calculation logic together, so reports can work from consistent measures and agreed analytical structures.

Reporting suite

Executive, commercial or operational views designed around the questions each audience needs to answer, with purposeful navigation and readable visual hierarchy.

Routes from a headline number to the detail behind it

Defined routes from a high-level exception to the underlying period, product, customer or operating unit, with context preserved as the user investigates.

Proof the reports reconcile, and who can see them

Reconciliation evidence, test scenarios and documented access rules so users and owners understand how the reporting has been checked and shared.

Adoption and reporting guide

Practical guidance on interpreting the reports, maintaining key definitions and running the management discussions and follow-up actions they are intended to support.

Common questions
Can you improve reports we already use?

Yes. The starting point is the business questions, existing definitions, source data and the current reporting experience.

Is this just building dashboards?

The work includes metric definitions, data models, report design, validation and adoption. The dashboard is one part of that system.

How do we know the reports are useful?

We agree with you, before building, who will use the reports, which decisions they support and how we will judge success. After launch we check whether they are being used that way.
