# Pattern Grid website design prompt

This is the master creative and implementation brief for a complete Pattern Grid website. It defines the site architecture, proposed public copy, visual system, imagery, interactions, assessment and delivery requirements. Use it as one prompt in a capable website builder, or give it to a design and development team as the governing brief.

The creative direction is **The Architecture of Intelligence**. The site should let visitors see fragmented business information become a reliable system for decisions. Its distinction should come from the quality of that transformation, the precision of its typography and the care in every interaction.

## 01 Assignment and creative ambition

Act as a senior creative director, enterprise brand strategist, editorial designer, interaction designer, creative developer and B2B conversion writer. Design a highly modern, multipage website for **Pattern Grid**, an emerging African Data, AI and Business Intelligence consultancy serving African and international organisations.

Create an original visual identity for the website within the existing Pattern Grid brand. Combine the following qualities as creative references, without reproducing another company's layouts, assets, logos, language or trade dress.

| Reference | Quality to translate into Pattern Grid |
| --- | --- |
| Apple | Immediate understanding, disciplined hierarchy, generous space and visual demonstrations that explain the proposition. |
| SpaceX | Cinematic framing, scale, purposeful movement and the feeling that complex engineering is working beneath a calm surface. |
| Palantir | Serious business questions, visible relationships between systems and analytical depth that earns technical confidence. |
| Rolls-Royce | Material quality, composure, exact alignment, deliberate pacing and small details that reward attention. |

Aim for the experience of a carefully engineered instrument presented in an architectural gallery. Let the visitor feel the complexity being resolved. Luxury should come from proportion, finish and confidence. Use Pattern Grid's navy, white and teal throughout; avoid ornamental gold or automotive imagery.

This is a consulting company website, not a software subscription product. Explain the expertise, work and engagements clearly. Make the visual experience ambitious while keeping navigation, reading and booking straightforward.

Build a genuine routed website. Each service and industry needs its own considered page. A homepage with anchor links does not fulfil this brief.

## 02 Business position and editorial rules

### Positioning

**Pattern Grid builds the data, analytics and AI systems behind better business decisions.**

We connect fragmented information, establish trusted metrics, automate repetitive work and help organisations put intelligence into everyday decisions.

The value proposition is a connected path from business questions to dependable data, useful analysis and practical action. Every page should make the relationship between these things clear.

### Audience and buying questions

| Audience | The question the website should answer |
| --- | --- |
| CEO and managing director | Can this firm help us see the business clearly and make better decisions? |
| CFO and finance leader | Can we reconcile our numbers, trust our metrics and reduce reporting effort? |
| COO and operations leader | Can we connect processes, identify exceptions and act earlier? |
| CIO, CTO and Head of Data | Can this firm design an appropriate architecture and make it maintainable? |
| Commercial and strategy leaders | Can we understand customers, channels, performance and growth opportunities? |

Use this order in public copy: **business question, desired outcome, consulting approach, enabling technology**. Mention Power BI, SQL, Microsoft Fabric, Azure and other tools where they explain the solution. Keep tools out of the primary proposition.

Use clear international English with consistent British spelling. Prefer concrete verbs: connect, define, reconcile, trace, compare, automate, forecast, investigate and decide. Keep most paragraphs to two or three sentences. Explain technical terms on first use.

Avoid generic promises such as “unlock the power of data”, “turn insights into impact”, “revolutionise your business”, “AI-powered everything”, “world-class solutions” and “end-to-end digital transformation”. Do not claim market leadership, guaranteed returns, universal real-time data, enterprise clients or scale that the supplied evidence does not establish.

### Evidence and public content

The supplied business brief supports the company positioning, capability areas, target industries and founder identity. Treat the copy in this document as proposed launch copy requiring normal business review.

Never invent client names, testimonials, outcomes, certifications, partnerships, employee numbers, office locations or years of experience. An anonymised client label is still a claim of client work and requires evidence.

Keep three content states separate in the CMS: verified client work, clearly labelled illustrative demonstrations, and unpublished drafts. Demonstrations must display “Illustrative scenario” above the title and “Sample data. Not client results.” beside any data visual. A demonstration must never become a case study simply by removing its label.

Do not fill missing evidence with public bracketed placeholders. Keep fields such as VERIFIED_CLIENT_OUTCOME and APPROVED_CONTACT_EMAIL in the internal content register. Hide unapproved modules or use the clearly labelled demonstration alternative described here.

## 03 Creative concept and visual grammar

### The Architecture of Intelligence

PATTERN represents a meaningful relationship in information. GRID represents the structure that makes the relationship usable.

Build a visual language around a precise grid gradually becoming legible. Begin with records that belong to different systems. Give them alignment, shared definitions and traceable connections. Resolve the composition into a business question with a clear answer and a visible source.

The signature object is **the Intelligence Grid**, a shallow, architectural assembly of small navy tiles and fine metallic tracks. It is a visual metaphor for connected systems, not a physical product Pattern Grid sells.

Art direct it as follows:

- A field of approximately 12 by 12 tiles, viewed at a restrained three-quarter angle. Small bevels catch soft light. The overall silhouette is low and architectural rather than a floating cube.
- Six subtly offset channels enter the structure, representing CRM, finance, ERP, sales, operations and customer data. Their labels remain crisp HTML or SVG overlays.
- Fine teal traces travel through the channels. Connections settle into an ordered matrix, then resolve into five outputs: trusted data, shared metrics, analysis, automation and decisions.
- Materials suggest matte ceramic, brushed aluminium and a small amount of translucent mineral glass. Keep reflections soft and physically plausible. Teal light should illuminate an edge, never bloom into neon fog.
- The camera moves only a few degrees. A quiet ground plane and soft shadow give the object weight. There are no orbiting planets, random particles, giant AI brains or decorative holograms.

Use this geometry across the website in different forms: a cinematic object in the hero, a readable diagram in the approach section, a fine grid in editorial covers and a small alignment detail in the footer. Do not repeat the same large render on every page.

### Three levels of visual density

**Cinematic moments:** the homepage hero, the central transformation sequence and one carefully chosen image-led section per detail page. These carry the most visual drama.

**Editorial sections:** services, industries, founder, insights and project narratives. Use generous white space, strong photography, rules and typography.

**Functional sections:** menus, forms, assessment questions and article reading. Prioritise clarity, reliable behaviour and quick response.

Never put all three levels at maximum intensity in the same viewport. The page should have a rhythm of spectacle, explanation and quiet.

Preserve navy, teal and white as the dominant identity. Aim for approximately 55–60% light surfaces, 30–35% navy and the remainder in restrained accents. These proportions guide the composition rather than impose a mathematical quota.

Use a navy-to-teal gradient only inside selected data objects or as a faint wash behind them. Flat colour should carry most of the site. No full-page mesh gradients, rainbow charts or an indiscriminate dark theme.

Do not use small white text on brand teal, or small brand-teal text on white. Use navy text on teal buttons, white text on navy, and deep teal for light-background links. Check final contrast for text, focus indicators, chart lines and controls; exact colour values alone do not prove accessibility.

### Typography

Use **Manrope** from Google Fonts as the primary website typeface. Use **IBM Plex Mono** from Google Fonts only for section indices, source labels, small diagram captions and carefully selected metrics.

This intentionally replaces Gotham for the website in response to the request for a modern Google Font. Preserve any existing Gotham-based logo artwork. Do not typeset the logo in a substitute font or silently change the wider corporate identity.

Manrope should feel composed and geometric, with medium-weight display text and highly readable body copy. The monospaced accent adds technical precision without turning the website into a terminal. Use two font families only.

| Role | Desktop size | Mobile size | Weight and treatment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Homepage display | 80–112 px | 42–56 px | Manrope 500, line height 1.04–1.08, tracking approximately -0.04em. |
| Detail page H1 | 64–88 px | 38–48 px | Manrope 500, line height 1.08, tracking -0.03em. |
| Section H2 | 44–60 px | 30–38 px | Manrope 500 or 600, line height 1.12. |
| H3 | 24–32 px | 22–26 px | Manrope 600, line height 1.2. |

Use fluid scaling between breakpoints and check real copy at 320 px. Do not force a display size when the headline cannot fit comfortably. Keep long-form text at approximately 60–72 characters per line. Use tabular numerals for aligned data. Never animate headlines character by character or replace letters with symbols.

Self-host the required font files where practical, retain their licence files, use font-display swap and preload only the essential face. Avoid loading unused weights, character sets or italics.

### Layout and spacing

Use a 12-column desktop grid with a maximum content width of 1440 px, 24–32 px gutters and responsive outer padding. At 1440 px, begin with 64 px outer margins. At 1920 px, expand the surrounding negative space rather than stretching paragraphs.

Use eight columns on tablet and four on mobile, with 20–24 px mobile gutters. Base spacing on 8 px increments, with 4 px permitted for optical corrections. Typical desktop section spacing is 112–160 px; mobile spacing is 64–88 px.

Use corners of 4–8 px for panels and controls. Reserve pills for taxonomy tags where useful. Use flat surfaces, fine borders and carefully placed shadows. Avoid a page made entirely of floating rounded cards.

### Component rules

Primary button on white: navy fill, white text. Primary button on navy: teal fill, navy text. Use the full label “Book a Data & AI Consultation” where space permits, and “Book a Consultation” in navigation. Buttons are at least 48 px high and have visible keyboard focus.

Secondary actions are text links with a small arrow, or a restrained outline button. On hover, the arrow may move 4 px and the background may shift slightly. Do not use magnetic buttons or controls that move away from the pointer.

Use editorial rows for services, image-led project features, substantial industry panels and quiet article listings. Do not reduce every content type to the same three-card component.

## 16 Motion direction and detailed storyboards

### Motion principles

Make motion explain connection, reconciliation, lineage, investigation and action. The site can be visually ambitious without keeping every element in motion. Concentrate the work into a few signature sequences, then let content settle.

Use a common entrance easing of cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1) and simple ease-in-out for repeating traces. Typical controls respond in 160–220 ms, component transitions take 300–500 ms, and major composition changes take 700–1100 ms. Avoid springy overshoot, bouncing type and cinematic delays on basic navigation.

Scroll progress should drive meaningful visual state, not text readability. Content must never remain invisible because an animation failed to initialise.

Do not add a smooth-scroll library merely for fashion.

Store public copy and page metadata separately from animation code. Reuse service, industry, project and article templates while preserving each page's content and diagram. The assessment must have explicit scoring logic, accessible results and tested state transitions.

### Performance budgets

Treat the following as design and engineering targets, not measurements already achieved.

- Target LCP at or below 2.5 seconds, INP at or below 200 ms and CLS at or below 0.1, measured at the 75th percentile in real use across mobile and desktop segments.
- Keep the critical initial route lightweight. Target no more than roughly 250 KB of compressed application JavaScript before optional 3D and below-the-fold features; document any justified exception.
- Aim for a mobile hero poster below 200 KB and a desktop poster below 350 KB after visual review. Use responsive AVIF or WebP variants, explicit dimensions and an appropriate preload for the actual largest image.
- Load optional 3D after the useful first screen. Set a target of roughly 1.5 MB or less for its initial compressed assets, including geometry and textures. Simplify the scene if quality and speed cannot coexist within the target.
- Do not ship a large autoplay hero video as the only way to see the visual. Avoid mandatory image-sequence downloads and full-resolution textures on small screens.
- Suspend rendering when the canvas is offscreen or the tab is hidden. Cap pixel density sensibly and reduce scene complexity when frame time degrades. Dispose of resources on route changes.

Use a capability ladder: static poster first; SVG enhancement for ordinary mobile devices; full 3D only when the viewport, preferences and measured runtime behaviour support it. Respect reduced motion and data-saving signals where available, but do not depend on browser-specific APIs as the only fallback. WebGL failure or context loss must return to the poster without breaking the page.

Test the main journeys on a representative mid-range Android device and constrained mobile network. Use lab checks before launch and field data when available. A single desktop Lighthouse score is not the acceptance criterion.

### Accessibility and functional requirements

Target WCAG 2.2 AA and adopt reduced-motion support in addition. Use semantic landmarks, a skip link, meaningful headings, descriptive links, labelled controls and visible focus. Ensure text meets applicable contrast requirements and chart meaning does not depend on colour alone.

All navigation, tabs, disclosures, forms and assessment steps must work by keyboard. Manage focus on route changes and dialog openings. Restore focus when overlays close. Expose actual validation and submission states accessibly.

Decorative canvases are hidden from assistive technology and accompanied by an equivalent text explanation. Informative charts include a text summary or accessible table. Never embed essential copy inside an image or canvas.

Provide pause, stop or hide controls for qualifying automatic movement that continues beyond five seconds. Avoid flashing. Respect reduced motion before animation starts. Keep controls at least 44 by 44 px as a project touch-target standard, with adequate separation.

### Content and integration model

Use structured content types for services, industries, projects, insights, people and site settings. Project fields include content type, evidence status, approval status, image rights, metric basis and publication date where relevant. Keep internal evidence notes private.

Enquiry submission requires server-side validation, rate limiting and a confirmed destination. Keep secrets off the client. Use progressive spam protection without creating unnecessary friction. Do not implement a fake contact success response.

For analytics and third-party tools, minimise collected information and publish notices that reflect the final implementation. Determine any cookie-consent requirements from the actual tools and jurisdictions. Do not use generic legal copy as a substitute for the company's approved policies.
