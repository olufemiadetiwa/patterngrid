# Pattern Grid website visual upgrade prompt

Use this as the second design pass in the existing Pattern Grid website project. It replaces the earlier instructions for typography, hero design, imagery, page composition and animation. Retain the established business scope, routes, verified content, assessment logic and working forms. Where visual instructions conflict, this document takes precedence.

The result should feel cinematic, contemporary and carefully art directed: large photographic scenes, decisive typography, short headlines and motion that makes business intelligence understandable. Complete the visual upgrade across the whole website, including mobile.

## 01 Creative direction

Act as a senior digital creative director, photographic art director, typography specialist and creative developer. Redesign Pattern Grid as a premium Data, AI and Business Intelligence consultancy serving African and international organisations.

The creative idea is **Intelligence in the real world**. Show the environments where business decisions have consequences: goods moving through a warehouse, a financial district beginning its day, teams examining a question, the precision of engineered infrastructure. Connect these scenes to Pattern Grid's expertise through the copy and the interactions beneath them.

Translate Apple into clarity and confident scale; SpaceX into cinematic composition and purposeful movement; Palantir into serious analytical thinking; and Rolls-Royce into precision, restraint and exceptional finish. Create an original Pattern Grid expression. Do not copy these companies' assets, layouts, logos or signature product imagery.

The first viewport must immediately communicate design quality. Use a striking full-width image, one large headline and carefully positioned navigation. The following section must explain the business clearly. The visitor should understand within the opening two sections that Pattern Grid connects data, analytics and AI to business decisions.

Make these changes in order:

1. Replace the opening grid sculpture with a cinematic photographic slider. Give every page its own image sequence.
2. Replace Manrope and the pervasive IBM Plex Mono treatment with the typography system below.
3. Remove paragraphs, diagnostic labels, diagrams and multiple competing actions from hero images. Keep the hero headline short and bold.
4. Add one large statement directly beneath each hero, with words gaining emphasis as the visitor scrolls.
5. Recompose repeated card grids into a varied editorial layout with larger images and more open space.
6. Replace generic entrance effects with coordinated image transitions, meaningful data demonstrations and precise interaction states.

The finished site must look considered in a still screenshot as well as in motion. Animation cannot compensate for a weak photograph or an indecisive type hierarchy.

## 02 Typography and visual system

### Font decision

Use **Geist**, available through Google Fonts, as the primary family for headings, body copy, navigation and buttons. Its direct, precise character suits this direction. The recommended shortlist is Geist, Inter and Instrument Sans; these are design recommendations, not a claimed global popularity ranking.

| Font | Role in this decision |
| --- | --- |
| Geist | Selected. Use strong display weights and restrained body weights to unify the entire site. |
| Inter | Alternative when maximum familiarity and interface readability are the priority. Use instead of Geist, not alongside it. |
| Instrument Sans | Alternative for a slightly more editorial character. Use instead of Geist if selected later. |

Do not mix all three families. Reserve Geist Mono, if needed, for actual data values, code samples and a few technical annotations. Use the primary sans serif for section labels and navigation. Typography should feel like a brand rather than a developer console.

| Element | Desktop starting point | Mobile starting point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Home hero headline | 112–152 px, weight 650–700, line height 0.98 | 48–68 px, weight 650–700, line height 1.02 |
| Inner page hero headline | 88–120 px, weight 650 | 44–60 px, weight 650 |
| Statement below hero | 48–64 px, weight 500–550, line height 1.15 | 28–36 px, weight 500–550, line height 1.2 |
| Section heading | 48–72 px, weight 600 | 32–42 px, weight 600 |
| Body text | 18–20 px, weight 400, line height 1.6 | 17–18 px, weight 400, line height 1.6 |
| Navigation and controls | 14–16 px, weight 500 | 15–17 px, weight 500 |

These are starting values, not rigid sizes. Use fluid sizing. Keep hero tracking around minus 0.035 em, adjusting by eye for the actual phrase; keep body tracking normal. Avoid compressed letters, overly thin headlines, tiny navigation and excessive all-caps text. Inspect every headline at 360 px width and 200 percent zoom. Never force a long word outside the viewport to preserve an intended line break.

Use the existing brand navy **#02203D**, deep ink **#07131C**, warm white **#F5F5F1**, pure white **#FFFFFF** and teal **#38A0C3**. Teal is a selective accent for a trace, a selected state or one emphasised phrase. It should not tint every image or appear as a gradient across every headline. Use a darker teal, such as **#17647D**, where coloured text on a light background needs stronger contrast.

Alternate dark cinematic moments with generous light editorial sections. Use a 12-column desktop grid, approximately 1440 px maximum content width, 64–88 px desktop gutters and 20–24 px mobile gutters. Images may extend to the viewport edge. Most text should remain inside the content grid. Body paragraphs should usually stay within 55–70 characters per line.

Use 120–176 px between major desktop sections and 64–88 px on mobile. Let content determine the final spacing. Prefer square or subtly rounded image corners, fine rules and direct alignment. Avoid a continuous sequence of rounded cards, shaded panels, floating pills, enormous shadows and decorative glass effects.

## 03 Photography and image quality

Make authentic photography the dominant visual language. Use commissioned photographs, supplied approved images or carefully selected licensed photographs. Aim for roughly four photographic placements for each abstract or diagrammatic placement. Data diagrams must be drawn accurately in code or vector form.

Choose images with a clear subject, strong geometry, realistic light and enough quiet space for the headline. The subject should feel observed rather than staged for a corporate brochure. Build a coherent collection with controlled highlights, deep but readable shadows, neutral whites and restrained blue or teal accents already present in the scene.

Represent African business with contemporary specificity and professional range. Verify the location before labelling a photograph as Lagos or another place. Avoid tourist shorthand, hardship imagery and interchangeable stock representations of the continent. Stock photography must not imply that a depicted office, team or facility belongs to Pattern Grid or one of its clients.

### Three image families

**Business at scale.** Use architectural photography, ports, distribution centres, retail environments and infrastructure. Compose around repeating lines, movement, routes and relationships. These images establish the scale of the decisions the firm helps organisations make.

**People exercising judgement.** Use real, candid professional moments with natural expression and believable environments. Show attention, discussion and work. Avoid handshakes, people pointing at empty glass, staged boardroom applause and exaggerated laptop reactions. Use only approved real photographs of the founder; never invent her likeness.

**Precision and structure.** Use real optical instruments, engineered materials, cable paths, light through architectural glass and carefully photographed structural details. Use these sparingly as visual metaphors. They should retain texture and believable physics.

### Selection and crop standard

For each candidate photograph, check the original at full size. Reject distorted hands, repeated faces, implausible reflections, unreadable screen gibberish, invented logos, warped buildings, plastic skin and unnecessary futuristic overlays. Avoid an image merely because it is available or matches the brand colour.

Do not use AI-generated people, synthetic boardrooms, glowing brains, holographic globes, levitating cubes or luminous circuit clouds as hero content. If an abstract visual is commissioned or generated, use it only as an intentional artwork with credible materials and a clear role. Do not present it as a real operation or client site.

Provide separate desktop and mobile crops, not just a universal centre crop. For text placed left, favour visual interest on the right and a quiet left third. Preserve faces, hands and the essential context on mobile. Inspect the complete slider sequence for consistent exposure and colour before approving individual frames.

Apply local gradients where needed for legibility. Do not cover every image with a heavy navy wash. Keep the photograph visible and compelling. Text must remain readable throughout every crossfade, not only at the start and end.

## 04 Hero slider on every page

Create one reusable hero component with page-specific imagery and headline content. The home page uses three images. Every other page uses two distinct images; two crops of the same frame do not count as two images. Asset reuse across secondary slides is acceptable when relevant, but the main marketing pages should each have a distinctive opening image.

Keep **one stable headline** over the changing images. This gives each page a clear identity and prevents important copy disappearing before it is read. Image changes provide the cinematic movement. There must be one semantic H1 per page. Do not create hidden duplicate H1 elements for each slide.

The hero contains only the headline, global navigation and discreet slider controls. Put breadcrumbs or page-category labels below the image when orientation is needed. Do not place explanatory paragraphs, bullet lists, statistics, technology logos or stacked CTA buttons over the photograph. Place the main page CTA beneath the scroll statement. The global “Book a consultation” link remains available in navigation.

### Composition and sizes

**Home:** use approximately 88–96 svh on desktop, with 680–980 px as a starting range. Place the headline in the lower-left area, about 64–88 px from the edge and well above the slider controls. Prefer two intentional lines: “See what” and “matters.” On mobile use approximately 72–82 svh with content-safe padding and no rigid minimum height that traps users on short screens.

**Marketing and service pages:** use approximately 72–84 svh on desktop and 64–74 svh on mobile. Give all pages a strong opening without making every page the exact same composition. Change subject scale and focal point while retaining the shared type and control system.

**Contact and assessment:** use a more compact 48–60 svh opening. Their forms and next steps must be easy to reach. Stop slider autoplay when the user begins an assessment or focuses a form field.

**Articles, case studies and legal pages:** keep the image slider, using a compact 36–48 svh presentation and manual navigation. Put long article titles in a solid editorial band immediately beneath the images rather than shrinking them over a photograph. Their title band is the H1; do not add a second headline to the image. Legal pages can use their short H1 directly on the image.

### Motion and controls

Use a slow, high-quality crossfade of approximately 1100 ms. Let each still image gently scale from 1.00 to no more than 1.04 during its eight-second display. Keep the focal point consistent and avoid cropping movement that appears accidental. The headline remains still during slide changes. On initial entry it can reveal once with a short vertical mask and a 12–18 px movement over 650–800 ms.

On marketing pages, begin autoplay only when the hero is visible. Provide previous, next and pause/play buttons with clear labels and visible focus states. Use a discreet “01 / 03” indicator or segmented progress line at the bottom-right. Controls must be at least 44 by 44 CSS pixels, even if their icons are smaller. On mobile keep controls clear of the headline, safe areas and browser chrome.

Pause on hover, keyboard focus within the hero, a hidden browser tab and when the hero leaves the viewport. Explicit user pause persists across routes for that session. After a manual slide selection, do not unexpectedly restart autoplay. Support touch swipes without blocking vertical page scrolling. Slider controls must work without hover.

Keep inactive images and slide controls out of the accessibility reading order as appropriate. Decorative images use empty alt text; meaningful images receive concise descriptions. Announce manual slide changes without repeatedly announcing autoplay. Do not transfer focus when a slide changes.

For reduced motion, show the first image immediately, disable autoplay and zoom, and keep manual controls available with an immediate swap or very brief fade. Never require animation to see the headline or reach the page content.

## 06 Text highlighting on scroll

Place the page's statement immediately beneath the hero on a generous solid background. This should become a recognisable Pattern Grid signature: a large paragraph whose words become more emphatic as the visitor moves through it.

Use one statement of approximately 20–40 words on marketing pages. Start with a readable muted colour, then progressively fill the text from left to right and line to line. On warm white, begin around **#646B70** and finish in **#07131C**. On deep ink, begin around **#9AA4AC** and finish in white. Verify actual rendered contrast for both states. The user must be able to read the full statement before the animation completes.

Emphasise one or two key phrases with the darker teal on light backgrounds or brand teal on dark backgrounds. Avoid highlighting every noun. Use a colour fill or a restrained underline; avoid blocks of marker colour behind whole paragraphs.

Start progression when the statement enters the lower portion of the viewport and complete it before it reaches the upper quarter. Map progress to the visible passage through the viewport, not a long artificial scroll distance. If the statement exceeds the screen height, use line-level observation so every line can complete. On scroll reversal, the effect may reverse smoothly; it must not flash or re-run a timed entrance.

Keep the statement in normal document flow. Do not trap the visitor in a pinned text section. Use one semantic paragraph with inline spans, or a carefully aligned decorative overlay marked aria-hidden. Preserve natural spaces, selection, search and screen-reader reading order. Do not announce individual words.

The base state without JavaScript must be fully readable. With reduced motion, show the complete emphasis immediately. Recalculate line layout after fonts load or the viewport changes. Do not split text into fixed line boxes that break on mobile. Use this effect once in the opening of each page; leave ordinary paragraphs, legal text, forms and article bodies static.

## 08 Animation and interaction direction

The upgrade needs a small number of memorable, well-executed sequences supported by excellent everyday interactions. Give the highest visual intensity to the hero and the signature analytical demonstration. Surround them with calmer reading sections.

| Interaction | Behaviour | Timing and restraint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hero images | Crossfade with a minimal slow push | About 1100 ms transition and eight-second dwell; never move the headline with every slide |
| Opening headline | One short masked reveal by line | 650–800 ms, 80 ms stagger at most; no typewriter or letter scramble |
| Image entering view | Reveal through a simple rectangular clip, with a slight settling movement | 700–900 ms once; no spinning frames or excessive blur |
| Section entrance | Opacity and 16–24 px vertical movement | 450–650 ms once; do not hide the whole page until scrolling |
| Service selection | Image crossfade and active row emphasis | 300–450 ms; equally usable by click, focus and touch |
| Image link hover | Very slight crop zoom and short arrow movement | 250–350 ms; no magnetic cursor or large tilt |
| Navigation panel | Clean opacity and small vertical movement | 180–240 ms; focus managed correctly |
| Route change | A short content fade with correct scroll and focus behaviour | 150–250 ms maximum; no full-screen loading interlude |
| Data explanation | A meaningful relationship becomes visible | Controlled by the section's progress; readable labels remain stable |

Use a consistent eased finish, such as cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1), for entrances. Use a smooth ease-in-out for crossfades. Avoid overshoot, springs, bouncing and constant floating objects. Do not use scrolling logos, flashing highlights, random particles, fake live counters or looping number changes.

### Signature sequence for the home page
